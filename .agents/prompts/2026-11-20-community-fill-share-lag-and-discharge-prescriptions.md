# Session: a community fill is drawn -- whether it happens and how long after; discharge prescriptions join it (ADR, next free number)

Context. Under `:dispense`, every order an outpatient visit wrote is filled once, `:community`, at the instant the visit closes (ADR-0187). dense-7500 now holds that population: 7,791 community fills at 28,024 arrivals. Two things are unrealistic and one population is missed. Every prescription is filled; every fill is instantaneous; and an order written during an admission that is still active and undispensed when the stay closes gets nothing -- the `injuries` module's 210 orders at the instant of discharge are the measured case (`.agents/session-records/2026-11-20-dense-outpatient-pathways.md`, step 4). This session makes the fill a draw (a share that is filled, and a lag), both as config options with provisional defaults, and treats an undispensed order at discharge as a discharge prescription under the same two options. Refills are NOT in this session. Stability across revisions is relaxed by standing ruling (ADR-0189); determinism at one revision is not. Author ruling, verbatim, 2026-11-20: "Accept all, go." -- to these defaults and this scope: "Share of prescriptions filled 0.80"; "Lag from visit close to first fill: 1 hour to 7 days, uniform"; and "Session A: the fill share and lag, plus discharge prescriptions. An order written in an admission that is still active and undispensed at discharge gets a community fill under the same two options." Standing: frequencies are options in the site config with reasonable defaults. (The same ruling accepted a refill interval and a continuation chance; those are the next session's.)

Channel facts for the defaults (read 2026-11-20; cite as the basis, call the defaults provisional):
- 78% of 195,930 e-prescriptions were filled; 72% of those for new medications (Fischer et al., J Gen Intern Med 2010;25(4):284-90, PMID 20131023).
- 17% of 150,565 new prescriptions in primary care were never filled (Tamblyn et al., CMAJ; https://cmaj.ca/node/149155).
- Of 32,586 new chronic-medication orders in an elderly US cohort, 75% were filled within 7 days and 81% within 30 (Franklin et al., J Gen Intern Med 2018, PMC6206334).
- After discharge from a general medicine service, 28% of patients with new prescriptions had not filled one or more at 7 days (PMC3642181).
- A uniform lag is cruder than these: real fills cluster early with a long tail. Say so in the docs.

Channel expectations (not rulings; correct from the tree and report):
- Two facility-level options beside the wards, since a community pharmacy belongs to no ward: a fill share in [0, 1] and a lag range in minutes, defaults 0.80 and [60 10080], validated as `:dispense-minutes` is. The executor names them in the tree's own style; dense-7500 writes its values out.
- Both draws are on the patient's `:pharmacy` stream, in a fixed order and number per order whether or not the fill happens, so one order's outcome moves no other's draw.
- Mechanism: where `decide :outpatient-visit-end` queues its fills today, it queues each at the close plus its drawn lag, or not at all. The queued step carries what it needs to decline later. When it is decided: no fill if the order has since ended. A community fill needs no open encounter; if the patient is inside a LATER encounter when it fires, the fill must not take that encounter's `:encounter-id` (ADR-0187's second finding, now reachable -- measure it, and make it impossible if the tree allows it cheaply).
- A fill queued for a patient later absorbed by a merge dies, as the inpatient one does. Count it.
- Discharge prescriptions: at `decide :discharge`, each of the patient's orders written in that stay that is still active and has no dispense is treated exactly as a visit's order is at its close: the same share, the same lag, `:setting :community`. An order the stay ended, or one already dispensed `:inpatient`, gets nothing.
- No event-schema change. A lag past the run's last event is not emitted; report how many fall off the end.
