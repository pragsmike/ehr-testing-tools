# Session: chronic orders refill; a pending fill follows its patient through a merge (ADR, next free number; event schema 1.13.0)

Context. ADR-0190 made a community fill a draw: a share that is filled and a lag. Two things remain. (1) Every prescription is filled at most once; a long-term medication is refilled. (2) A fill queued for a patient later absorbed by a merge dies with the patient's other queued steps -- 224 visit prescriptions at 28,024, which pulls the filled share to 0.765 against a configured 0.80. A merge corrects a record; the person still collects the medication. This session adds a chronic marker on a medication order, a fill number on a dispense, a refill chain for chronic orders bounded by a configured tail, and carries pending pharmacy steps to the surviving record at a merge. Stability across revisions is relaxed by standing ruling (ADR-0189); determinism at one revision is not. Author rulings, verbatim. 2026-11-20, "Accept all, go.", to defaults including: "Refill interval for chronic orders: 28 to 35 days, uniform" and "Chance each refill is followed by another: 0.94". 2026-11-21, "1. Yes. 2. tail. 3. ok.", to: (1) "Pending fills follow the survivor through a merge? I recommend yes, for both settings."; (2) "A configured tail (recommended). Refills may continue for a set number of days after the last arrival, as a config option. I'd default it to 90"; (3) "an optional `:chronic true` on a medication order, carried from modules and authorable in a pathway, and a `:fill-number` on every dispense, starting at 1. That takes the event schema to 1.13.0, additively." Also ruled there: a prescription whose first fill never happens gets no refills; refills for chronic orders that predate the horizon stay owed.

Channel facts for the defaults (cite as the basis, call them provisional): a 30-day supply is the traditional retail unit, and 90-day fills ran at 12-15% across four drug classes in a post-infarction cohort of 353,259 patients (https://scholars.duke.edu/individual/pub1470204); about half of patients who start a chronic medication discontinue it (Franklin et al., J Gen Intern Med 2018, PMC6206334, background) -- 0.94 compounded over twelve refills is about one half.

Channel expectations (not rulings; correct from the tree and report):
- Marker: the loader declares `chronic`, the compile carries it, the IR step and the `:medication-order` event take an optional `:chronic true` (absent means not chronic; never `false` written out), the order's record keeps it. `dense-clinic-chronic` states it. `:fill-number` is required on every `:medication-dispense`, authored, inpatient and community alike; 1 for a first dispense.
- Chain: when a `:community` dispense of a chronic order is emitted, two draws on the patient's `:pharmacy` stream -- continue or not (0.94), and the interval (28-35 days) -- in a fixed number per fill. If it continues, the next fill is queued at this fill's time plus the interval, with the next number. When it fires it declines if the order has ended. No share draw and no lag on a refill.
- Three new facility options beside the two community ones, named in the tree's style: the interval range, the continuation chance, the tail in days (default 90). The tail bounds the chain: no refill is queued for a time later than the last arrival's time plus the tail.
- Inpatient dispenses do not refill. A chronic order written in an admission and filled as a discharge prescription does.
- Merge: pending `:pharmacy-dispense` steps of the absorbed patient are re-queued on the survivor as ADR-0179 re-queues a `:result-followup`, both settings, the dispense law resolving the order through the merge as the end's law already does.
- The dispense law gains nothing new for refills beyond what it holds; a new invariant states the fill numbers of one order are 1..n without gaps and in time order.
